Scenario: The Car

Imagine you are driving a car along a perfectly flat road. The position of the car is at $x$ meters, and the height of the car is modeled by the blue function $f(x)$ above.

We know the car will be on the ground ($f(x) = 0$) for all $x \neq 5$.

However, the camera (inconveniently) malfunctions when the car is at $x = 5$.

What do you expect the car's height to be at $x = 5$ (in meters)?

The Actual Value

The limit tells us what height we expect to be at based on the surrounding path. But the exact moment your front tires roll onto $x = 5$, ZAP!

The portal triggers instantly, teleporting your car exactly 1 meter up into the air.

$$f(5) = 1\text{ meter}$$

A split-second later, at $x = 5.01$, you are past the portal and back on the ground at 0 meters.

Compare the position of the car at 5 meters vs your prediction. Was it the same?



The Calculus Takeaway

The limit looks at the journey, while the actual value looks at the destination. Because the journey of the car from both sides of x = 5 meters expected the ground, the limit is 0. But because of the portal, the actual value is 1.

More generally, we can say that a limit is the value that a function approaches as the input gets closer and closer to a specific point.

Take a look at the graph below: try zooming in to x = 0 to see what happens.

What does the function approach as x approaches 0?

$$\lim_{x \to 5} f(x) = 0 \quad \neq \quad f(5) = 1$$

In calculus, this is called a removable discontinuity. The road has a perfectly predictable trend, but a single point has been "removed" and popped up into the air!

This illustrates a key concept: a function's limit at a point can exist even if the function's value at that exact point is different or undefined.
